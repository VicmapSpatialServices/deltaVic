VLRS client

GUI guide

The VLRS client copies Vicmap layers from VLRS into your own PostGIS database and keeps them current. Running python deltaVic.py with no arguments opens this window. Each screen below has numbered pointers. Hover over or tab to a note to highlight what it points at.

First run, in order

Nothing installed yet? Start with Install and first run.

  1. On Setup & QA, enter your email and press Refresh. VLRS issues a client ID and emails you a validation link.
  2. Click the link in that email, then press Refresh again. Both registration checks turn green and the Meta tab fills in.
  3. Enter your database details and press Test database until all four checks pass.
  4. On Meta, tick the layers you want kept in sync.
  5. Back on Setup & QA, press SYNC. A first sync downloads every ticked layer in full, so it takes a while.
  6. Schedule a sync every hour so the layers stay current. The window only syncs when you press SYNC. Install and first run shows how for Windows, macOS and Linux.

Setup & QA

Where the client learns who you are and where your database lives. Work down it once on a new machine. After that it is mostly the Replication panel and the SYNC button.

  1. 1

    Tabs

    Setup & QA is where you configure and run the client. Meta is the catalogue of layers VLRS publishes. Data is the tables already in your local database.

  2. 2

    Profile and API

    Each profile is a section of config.ini with its own registration and database. Switching profile reloads every field below. API shows the VLRS endpoint that profile talks to.

  3. 3

    Registration

    Your email, and the client ID and API key VLRS issued when you registered. The ID and key are only ever shown masked, and are held in config.ini.

  4. 4

    Registration checks

    Refresh sends your email to VLRS. The first time, that registers you and VLRS emails a validation link. Once you have clicked it, Refresh ticks Validated and loads your read and upload rights. It runs by itself at start up.

  5. 5

    Database connection

    The PostGIS database the layers are copied into. Bin path is the folder holding pg_dump and pg_restore, which the client runs to load each supply.

  6. 6

    Test database

    Saves the fields to config.ini, then checks four things in order: that it can connect, that the PostgreSQL client tools are there, that PostGIS is installed, and that the vm_meta tables exist. It stops at the first failure and marks what it could not reach with a dash.

  7. 7

    Replication counts

    How many layers your database tracks, how many are switched on to sync, and how many are stuck in error. They read N/A until the database checks pass.

  8. 8

    Fix errors

    Clears the error on every failed layer and queues it for a full reload on the next sync. Nothing downloads until you press SYNC.

  9. 9

    SYNC

    Brings every active layer up to the latest supply, downloading and applying each one in turn until nothing is left to do. The button reads SYNCING while it works and is greyed out until the database is ready. It only runs when pressed, so keep the layers current with an hourly scheduled sync.

  10. 10

    Status bar

    The result of whatever you last did, and a moving bar while a sync or upload runs. Errors show here in red.

Meta: choosing layers

The catalogue of what VLRS publishes, grouped by schema. This is where you decide which layers your database keeps in sync.

  1. 1

    Schemas

    Pick one to list its layers. A schema is coloured by the contract most of its layers belong to.

  2. 2

    Key

    Shows or hides what the colours mean. VLAT is the address and property contract, VTT the topographic one, and MISC is maintained in house and is the only kind you can upload.

  3. 3

    Layers

    Every layer in the schema, with the count in the heading. Click a name to see its details below. The list scrolls with the mouse wheel.

  4. 4

    Sync checkbox

    Ticked layers are included in a sync, unticked ones are left alone. The change is saved to your database at once. The boxes stay greyed out until the database checks on Setup & QA pass.

  5. 5

    All on, All off

    Ticks or clears every layer in the selected schema in one go.

  6. 6

    Layer details

    Empty until you pick a layer. The next figure shows it filled in.

Meta: a layer in detail

Clicking a layer fetches what VLRS knows about it: its structure and every supply it has had.

  1. 1

    Selected layer

    Highlighted in the list, and named as schema.table at the top of the details.

  2. 2

    Reseed

    Queues this one layer for a full reload. Use it when a local copy has drifted or been edited. The reload happens on the next SYNC.

  3. 3

    Metadata weblink

    Opens the layer's ISO 19115 record in GeoNetwork in your browser.

  4. 4

    Columns

    The table's columns and their PostgreSQL types.

  5. 5

    Indexes

    Each index with the column it covers. In the Type column P marks the primary key and U a unique index.

  6. 6

    Update history

    Every supply VLRS has published for the layer, newest first. Type is inc for an incremental change or full for a complete reload. Adds and Dels are the rows each one changed, and Count is the table's size afterwards.

Data: your local tables

What is actually in your database. It lists every schema there, including ones that did not come from Vicmap, and it is where MISC layers are uploaded back to VLRS.

  1. 1

    Local schemas

    The schemas in your database. Ones VLRS does not know, such as topology or your own work, are listed too.

  2. 2

    Local layer

    Click a table to see its state and upload options. There are no sync boxes on this tab.

  3. 3

    Upload

    Sends your copy of a MISC layer to VLRS as a new supply. DIFF sends the changes, FULL the whole table. A table VLRS has never seen can only go FULL, and INC is not available from the window. The buttons are greyed out when your registration has no write rights on the layer, and always for VLAT and VTT.

  4. 4

    What an upload will do

    One line saying whether VLRS already holds the table, or why it cannot be uploaded.

  5. 5

    Dataset

    How VLRS describes the table: table or view, its GeoNetwork record, its geometry type, and whether it goes to VDP. For a table VLRS already holds these are shown read only. For a new one the heading reads Register and upload, and you fill them in before a FULL upload.

  6. 6

    Local state

    What your database records about the table: its sync status, the contract and supply type it last loaded, the supply version, its row count, and whether it is switched on to sync. A status in error is shown in red.
